# Answer and Affirmative Defenses

## Matter Name

Harbor Fluid Systems v. Ironcrest Inspection Group

## Document Date

2025-05-01

## Document Type

Answer and Affirmative Defenses

## Page 1

### Caption

IN THE CIRCUIT COURT OF COOK COUNTY, ILLINOIS  
LAW DIVISION

IRONCREST INSPECTION GROUP, LLC  
Defendant, by counsel, answers the Complaint of HARBOR FLUID SYSTEMS, INC..

### General Response

1. Ironcrest admits that Elise Grant inspected Lot HF-144 on 2025-06-12.
2. Ironcrest admits that the inspection raised concerns and that the report later marked Exhibit 21 included hold language.
3. Ironcrest denies that it failed to communicate the inspection concern to Harbor.
4. Ironcrest alleges that Harbor quality and operations personnel were informed of the hold recommendation on 2025-06-12 and that Harbor nevertheless allowed Truck 5932 to depart.

## Page 2

### Specific Admissions and Denials

5. Ironcrest admits that Simon Wu was its primary quality contact at Harbor.
6. Ironcrest admits that Delta Marine Equipment was pressing for on-time delivery.
7. Ironcrest denies that the first meaningful communication of the hold occurred only after the truck departed.
8. Ironcrest states that Elise Grant verbally communicated a hold recommendation on 2025-06-12 and issued a preliminary written report that same evening.
9. Ironcrest denies that it had authority to physically prevent Harbor from shipping the lot once Harbor personnel chose to proceed.
10. Ironcrest further denies any allegation that the identified defects were hidden, trivial, or incapable of supporting a hold recommendation.

### Defendant's Position

11. Ironcrest contends that Harbor personnel knew enough by the close of business on 2025-06-12 to quarantine the lot.
12. Ironcrest further contends that any release of the truck on 2025-06-13 resulted from Harbor's own internal decision chain, customer pressure, and inadequate internal release controls.

## Page 3

### Affirmative Defenses

13. As a first affirmative defense, Ironcrest states that Harbor's damages were caused in whole or in part by Harbor's own acts or omissions, including the failure to maintain a reliable shipment hold process.
14. As a second affirmative defense, Ironcrest states that Harbor failed to mitigate damages after learning of the inspection concern.
15. As a third affirmative defense, Ironcrest states that intervening acts by Harbor operations personnel, including any undocumented verbal release, broke the chain of causation.
16. As a fourth affirmative defense, Ironcrest states that Harbor's own records reflect awareness of the hold recommendation before the truck departed.

### Prayer

17. Ironcrest requests judgment in its favor, dismissal of the Complaint with prejudice, costs, and any further relief deemed just.
