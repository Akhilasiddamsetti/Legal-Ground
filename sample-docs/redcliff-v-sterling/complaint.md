### Parties and Nature of Action

1. Plaintiff Redcliff Motion Works, Inc. manufactures actuator assemblies for industrial customers.
2. Defendant Sterling Plant Assurance, LLC provides third-party inspection and shipment-release services for industrial components.
3. This action arises from Sterling's inspection of Lot RM-511, a shipment of actuator assemblies prepared for delivery to Alpine Robotics.
4. Redcliff retained Sterling because Alpine Robotics required independent inspection before release.
5. Redcliff alleges that Sterling identified serious defects, prepared the report later marked as Exhibit 9, and failed to communicate the hold recommendation in a clear and timely manner to the people controlling shipment.

### Core Event

6. On 2025-07-10, Isabel Torres inspected Lot RM-511 at Redcliff Motion Plant 3.
7. The inspection identified a cracked actuator collar on sampled units, grease contamination inside the assembly cavity, and shaft runout exceeding tolerance.
8. Truck 4807 nevertheless departed Redcliff's dock on 2025-07-11 with the inspected lot.

### Factual Allegations

9. Owen Hartley was Plant Manager for Redcliff Motion Plant 3 and oversaw production and outbound staging for Lot RM-511.
10. Meera Nair was Redcliff's Director of Quality and Sterling's main quality contact.
11. Blake Sutton was Redcliff's Vice President of Operations and had authority over shipment scheduling and customer escalation issues.
12. Cara Fields supervised the warehouse dock where Truck 4807 was loaded.
13. Jonah West managed the Alpine Robotics account and relayed urgent customer pressure to keep the shipment on schedule.
14. Sterling prepared a written inspection report, identified by the parties as Exhibit 9, reflecting a failed inspection and a hold recommendation.
15. Redcliff alleges the final written report was not effectively delivered to the people who could stop the truck before departure.

### Timeline

- 2025-04-10: Redcliff schedules an independent pre-shipment inspection with Sterling.
- 2025-07-10: Isabel Torres inspects Lot RM-511 and prepares the report later referred to as Exhibit 9.
- 2025-07-11: Truck 4807 departs with Lot RM-511 despite the inspection concern.
- 2025-07-14: Redcliff holds an internal meeting to determine how the shipment was released.
- 2025-07-15: Alpine Robotics rejects the shipment after receiving defective units.

### Claims and Damage

16. Redcliff claims that Sterling negligently performed and communicated the inspection, failed to ensure its hold recommendation was delivered to the correct decision-makers, and caused Redcliff to suffer customer rejection, expedited replacement costs, internal rework costs, and business interruption.
17. Redcliff further alleges that, had Sterling timely and clearly escalated the failed inspection and Exhibit 9 hold recommendation, Truck 4807 would not have departed.
18. Alpine Robotics later reported field failures linked to a cracked actuator collar on sampled units, grease contamination inside the assembly cavity, and shaft runout exceeding tolerance.
19. Redcliff seeks damages, costs, and any additional relief allowed by law.

### Reference to Exhibit 9

20. Exhibit 9 is central to this dispute because it is the strongest written record of the inspection findings and the recommendation not to ship Lot RM-511 pending review.
