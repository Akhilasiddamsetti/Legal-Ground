# Answer and Affirmative Defenses

## Matter Name

Redcliff Motion Works v. Sterling Plant Assurance

## Document Date

2025-05-01

## Document Type

Answer and Affirmative Defenses

## Page 1

### Caption

IN THE CIRCUIT COURT OF COOK COUNTY, ILLINOIS  
LAW DIVISION

STERLING PLANT ASSURANCE, LLC  
Defendant, by counsel, answers the Complaint of REDCLIFF MOTION WORKS, INC..

### General Response

1. Sterling admits that Isabel Torres inspected Lot RM-511 on 2025-07-10.
2. Sterling admits that the inspection raised concerns and that the report later marked Exhibit 9 included hold language.
3. Sterling denies that it failed to communicate the inspection concern to Redcliff.
4. Sterling alleges that Redcliff quality and operations personnel were informed of the hold recommendation on 2025-07-10 and that Redcliff nevertheless allowed Truck 4807 to depart.

## Page 2

### Specific Admissions and Denials

5. Sterling admits that Meera Nair was its primary quality contact at Redcliff.
6. Sterling admits that Alpine Robotics was pressing for on-time delivery.
7. Sterling denies that the first meaningful communication of the hold occurred only after the truck departed.
8. Sterling states that Isabel Torres verbally communicated a hold recommendation on 2025-07-10 and issued a preliminary written report that same evening.
9. Sterling denies that it had authority to physically prevent Redcliff from shipping the lot once Redcliff personnel chose to proceed.
10. Sterling further denies any allegation that the identified defects were hidden, trivial, or incapable of supporting a hold recommendation.

### Defendant's Position

11. Sterling contends that Redcliff personnel knew enough by the close of business on 2025-07-10 to quarantine the lot.
12. Sterling further contends that any release of the truck on 2025-07-11 resulted from Redcliff's own internal decision chain, customer pressure, and inadequate internal release controls.

## Page 3

### Affirmative Defenses

13. As a first affirmative defense, Sterling states that Redcliff's damages were caused in whole or in part by Redcliff's own acts or omissions, including the failure to maintain a reliable shipment hold process.
14. As a second affirmative defense, Sterling states that Redcliff failed to mitigate damages after learning of the inspection concern.
15. As a third affirmative defense, Sterling states that intervening acts by Redcliff operations personnel, including any undocumented verbal release, broke the chain of causation.
16. As a fourth affirmative defense, Sterling states that Redcliff's own records reflect awareness of the hold recommendation before the truck departed.

### Prayer

17. Sterling requests judgment in its favor, dismissal of the Complaint with prejudice, costs, and any further relief deemed just.
