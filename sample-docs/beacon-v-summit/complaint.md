### Parties and Nature of Action

1. Plaintiff Beacon Process Controls, Inc. manufactures hydraulic control valves for industrial customers.
2. Defendant Summit Verification Services, LLC provides third-party inspection and shipment-release services for industrial components.
3. This action arises from Summit's inspection of Lot BX-318, a shipment of hydraulic control valves prepared for delivery to Riverside Power Systems.
4. Beacon retained Summit because Riverside Power Systems required independent inspection before release.
5. Beacon alleges that Summit identified serious defects, prepared the report later marked as Exhibit 18, and failed to communicate the hold recommendation in a clear and timely manner to the people controlling shipment.

### Core Event

6. On 2025-04-14, Aaron Bell inspected Lot BX-318 at Beacon Plant 2.
7. The inspection identified damaged spool seals, bronze particulate in the valve bodies, and pressure-port bore drift outside customer tolerance.
8. Truck 7721 nevertheless departed Beacon's dock on 2025-04-15 with the inspected lot.

## Page 2

### Factual Allegations

9. Lena Ortiz was Plant Manager for Beacon Plant 2 and oversaw production and outbound staging for Lot BX-318.
10. Priya Shah was Beacon's Director of Quality and Summit's main quality contact.
11. Evan Brooks was Beacon's Vice President of Operations and had authority over shipment scheduling and customer escalation issues.
12. Maya Chen supervised the warehouse dock where Truck 7721 was loaded.
13. Colin Reeves managed the Riverside Power Systems account and relayed urgent customer pressure to keep the shipment on schedule.
14. Summit prepared a written inspection report, identified by the parties as Exhibit 18, reflecting a failed inspection and a hold recommendation.
15. Beacon alleges the final written report was not effectively delivered to the people who could stop the truck before departure.

### Timeline

- 2025-04-10: Beacon schedules an independent pre-shipment inspection with Summit.
- 2025-04-14: Aaron Bell inspects Lot BX-318 and prepares the report later referred to as Exhibit 18.
- 2025-04-15: Truck 7721 departs with Lot BX-318 despite the inspection concern.
- 2025-04-17: Beacon holds an internal meeting to determine how the shipment was released.
- 2025-04-18: Riverside Power Systems rejects the shipment after receiving defective units.

## Page 3

### Claims and Damage

16. Beacon claims that Summit negligently performed and communicated the inspection, failed to ensure its hold recommendation was delivered to the correct decision-makers, and caused Beacon to suffer customer rejection, expedited replacement costs, internal rework costs, and business interruption.
17. Beacon further alleges that, had Summit timely and clearly escalated the failed inspection and Exhibit 18 hold recommendation, Truck 7721 would not have departed.
18. Riverside Power Systems later reported field failures linked to damaged spool seals, bronze particulate in the valve bodies, and pressure-port bore drift outside customer tolerance.
19. Beacon seeks damages, costs, and any additional relief allowed by law.

### Reference to Exhibit 18

20. Exhibit 18 is central to this dispute because it is the strongest written record of the inspection findings and the recommendation not to ship Lot BX-318 pending review.
