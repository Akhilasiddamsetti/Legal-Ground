# Answer and Affirmative Defenses

## Matter Name

Beacon Process Controls v. Summit Verification Services

## Document Date

2025-05-01

## Document Type

Answer and Affirmative Defenses

## Page 1

### Caption

IN THE CIRCUIT COURT OF COOK COUNTY, ILLINOIS  
LAW DIVISION

SUMMIT VERIFICATION SERVICES, LLC  
Defendant, by counsel, answers the Complaint of BEACON PROCESS CONTROLS, INC..

### General Response

1. Summit admits that Aaron Bell inspected Lot BX-318 on 2025-04-14.
2. Summit admits that the inspection raised concerns and that the report later marked Exhibit 18 included hold language.
3. Summit denies that it failed to communicate the inspection concern to Beacon.
4. Summit alleges that Beacon quality and operations personnel were informed of the hold recommendation on 2025-04-14 and that Beacon nevertheless allowed Truck 7721 to depart.

## Page 2

### Specific Admissions and Denials

5. Summit admits that Priya Shah was its primary quality contact at Beacon.
6. Summit admits that Riverside Power Systems was pressing for on-time delivery.
7. Summit denies that the first meaningful communication of the hold occurred only after the truck departed.
8. Summit states that Aaron Bell verbally communicated a hold recommendation on 2025-04-14 and issued a preliminary written report that same evening.
9. Summit denies that it had authority to physically prevent Beacon from shipping the lot once Beacon personnel chose to proceed.
10. Summit further denies any allegation that the identified defects were hidden, trivial, or incapable of supporting a hold recommendation.

### Defendant's Position

11. Summit contends that Beacon personnel knew enough by the close of business on 2025-04-14 to quarantine the lot.
12. Summit further contends that any release of the truck on 2025-04-15 resulted from Beacon's own internal decision chain, customer pressure, and inadequate internal release controls.

## Page 3

### Affirmative Defenses

13. As a first affirmative defense, Summit states that Beacon's damages were caused in whole or in part by Beacon's own acts or omissions, including the failure to maintain a reliable shipment hold process.
14. As a second affirmative defense, Summit states that Beacon failed to mitigate damages after learning of the inspection concern.
15. As a third affirmative defense, Summit states that intervening acts by Beacon operations personnel, including any undocumented verbal release, broke the chain of causation.
16. As a fourth affirmative defense, Summit states that Beacon's own records reflect awareness of the hold recommendation before the truck departed.

### Prayer

17. Summit requests judgment in its favor, dismissal of the Complaint with prejudice, costs, and any further relief deemed just.
