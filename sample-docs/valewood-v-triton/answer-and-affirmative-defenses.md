# Answer and Affirmative Defenses

## Matter Name

Valewood Industrial Products v. Triton Field Inspections

## Document Date

2025-05-01

## Document Type

Answer and Affirmative Defenses

## Page 1

### Caption

IN THE CIRCUIT COURT OF COOK COUNTY, ILLINOIS  
LAW DIVISION

TRITON FIELD INSPECTIONS, LLC  
Defendant, by counsel, answers the Complaint of VALEWOOD INDUSTRIAL PRODUCTS, INC..

### General Response

1. Triton admits that Marisol Vega inspected Lot VI-267 on 2025-08-19.
2. Triton admits that the inspection raised concerns and that the report later marked Exhibit 14 included hold language.
3. Triton denies that it failed to communicate the inspection concern to Valewood.
4. Triton alleges that Valewood quality and operations personnel were informed of the hold recommendation on 2025-08-19 and that Valewood nevertheless allowed Truck 7055 to depart.

## Page 2

### Specific Admissions and Denials

5. Triton admits that Rohan Iyer was its primary quality contact at Valewood.
6. Triton admits that Keystone Energy Controls was pressing for on-time delivery.
7. Triton denies that the first meaningful communication of the hold occurred only after the truck departed.
8. Triton states that Marisol Vega verbally communicated a hold recommendation on 2025-08-19 and issued a preliminary written report that same evening.
9. Triton denies that it had authority to physically prevent Valewood from shipping the lot once Valewood personnel chose to proceed.
10. Triton further denies any allegation that the identified defects were hidden, trivial, or incapable of supporting a hold recommendation.

### Defendant's Position

11. Triton contends that Valewood personnel knew enough by the close of business on 2025-08-19 to quarantine the lot.
12. Triton further contends that any release of the truck on 2025-08-20 resulted from Valewood's own internal decision chain, customer pressure, and inadequate internal release controls.

## Page 3

### Affirmative Defenses

13. As a first affirmative defense, Triton states that Valewood's damages were caused in whole or in part by Valewood's own acts or omissions, including the failure to maintain a reliable shipment hold process.
14. As a second affirmative defense, Triton states that Valewood failed to mitigate damages after learning of the inspection concern.
15. As a third affirmative defense, Triton states that intervening acts by Valewood operations personnel, including any undocumented verbal release, broke the chain of causation.
16. As a fourth affirmative defense, Triton states that Valewood's own records reflect awareness of the hold recommendation before the truck departed.

### Prayer

17. Triton requests judgment in its favor, dismissal of the Complaint with prejudice, costs, and any further relief deemed just.
