# Root Cause Memo

## Matter Name

ForgeLine Components v. Axis Industrial Audits

## Document Date

2025-05-26

## Document Type

Root Cause Memo

## Page 1

### Executive Summary

The available record shows that Lot FC-902 should have remained on hold after the 2025-05-20 inspection. Even so, Truck 6614 left the dock on 2025-05-21. The strongest written evidence of the inspection failure is Exhibit 7, supported by the email chain and the later customer rejection.

### Preliminary Root Causes

1. Customer pressure compressed the decision window.
2. Quality and operations did not share a single documented release workflow.
3. Dock staff relied on assumptions and incomplete records rather than a final written release.

## Page 2

### Evidence Comparison

- Exhibit 7 and the Nonconformance Report describe the defects as serious enough to justify a hold.
- The complaint emphasizes delayed communication by Axis, while the answer argues ForgeLine knew enough on 2025-05-20 to stop the shipment.
- The interrogatory responses say Kyle Mercer did not review Exhibit 7 before departure, but Kyle later testified that the first page was seen on the evening of 2025-05-20.
- Sierra Vaughn recalls seeing a release printout listing Grant Holcomb as the operations approver, while the Corrective Action Plan notes a later print time and unresolved audit questions.

## Page 3

### Deposition Prep Topics and Gaps

- Pin down whether Anita Desai's early evening email was treated as a true stop-ship direction.
- Determine whether Grant Holcomb ever gave an actual verbal release or whether others inferred approval.
- Determine whether the approver field on the Shipment Release Form was entered live or backfilled later.
- Ask why the dock was allowed to continue staging after repeated hold references.

### Unresolved Conclusion

The record still does not identify the final approving person with confidence. Any assistant answer on that point should abstain rather than guess.
